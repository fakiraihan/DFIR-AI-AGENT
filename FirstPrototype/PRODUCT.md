# Product

## Register

product

## Users

JejakAgent is used by DFIR analysts, SOC operators, cybersecurity evaluators, and students working through incident-investigation evidence. They arrive with raw EVTX, LOG, TXT, or CSV security logs and need to turn noisy telemetry into a defensible investigation path, supported findings, and an exportable report.

## Product Purpose

JejakAgent automates the early and middle stages of cyber incident investigation. It parses uploaded logs, detects suspicious sequences with DeepLog, filters anomalies through a cybersecurity LLM gate, enriches indicators with threat-intelligence sources, and produces structured DFIR reports with evidence provenance, timeline reconstruction, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, limitations, and response recommendations.

Success means analysts can move from raw logs to an evidence-bound report faster, while still seeing enough intermediate state to understand, challenge, and validate the system's conclusions.

## Brand Personality

Professional, futuristic, and cybersecurity-centered. The interface should feel like a serious analyst workspace with advanced automation behind it: precise, alert, technical, and confident without slipping into gimmicky "AI" styling.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic blue AI-product styling: soft blue gradients, abstract AI glow, vague automation language, and visuals that could belong to any chatbot or SaaS dashboard. Avoid over-neon hacker-movie aesthetics, crypto-dashboard energy, and decorative effects that make evidence harder to read.

## Design Principles

Evidence first, spectacle second. Every screen should make investigation evidence, session state, confidence, and limitations easier to inspect before it adds visual drama.

Show the pipeline honestly. Users should always understand where the investigation is in the flow: upload, parsing, DeepLog detection, LLM gate, agent investigation, threat intelligence, and report generation.

Keep analyst trust visible. Surface provenance, uncertainty, severity, evidence IDs, export options, and failure states clearly so the system feels auditable rather than magical.

Use futurism with restraint. Cybersecurity atmosphere should come from dense information, precise motion, crisp contrast, and disciplined color, not from generic blue AI branding.

Design for repeated operational use. Prioritize scanability, keyboard-friendly controls, stable layout, readable tables, resilient long-text handling, and clear next actions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal accessibility standard is required for this prototype, but the interface should remain readable and usable: strong contrast for body text, visible focus states, non-color-only status cues, sensible reduced-motion behavior where practical, and layouts that preserve long evidence strings without overlap.
